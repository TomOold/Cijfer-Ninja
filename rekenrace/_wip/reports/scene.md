# Report: scène (RR.Scene)

## Summary
RR.Scene is done as parts/scene.js (about 2900 lines, plain IIFE, assigns RR.Scene only, no const RR, no globals except document/window/rAF used lazily). It draws a 320x144 side-view race on a canvas with eight distinct, hand-built pixel-art themes (tulips, windmill, beach, forest, canal, harbour, ice, hills). Each theme has a banded and ordered-dither sky, drifting clouds, 3-4 parallax layers, a road tile with lane dashes and kerbs, and recognisably Dutch details: striped bulb fields and a farm; windmills with turning lattice sails, ditches, cows and a dike; dunes, beach huts, a pavilion, sea, gulls and kites; pines, ferns, mushrooms, birches and dappled light; gabled canal houses with shopfronts, a bridge, bikes at the railing, reflections in the water and a moored boat; cranes, containers, boats, a lighthouse and wind turbines; a frozen canal with skaters, a koek-en-zopie stall with steam, snowy firs and a pale low sun; hills with vineyard terraces, a half-timbered farm and a castle on a hill. Everything is baked once per theme into cached offscreen canvases (bake takes about 10-35 ms, a frame about 0.1-0.8 ms), then only blitted. Karts are drawn in 4 depth lanes (player lane 0 last, in front) with a slight oblique projection: each lane further back sits 4 px to the right and the start/finish lines are slanted to match, so the 4 karts at the start line fan out and stay readable. A start gate with bunting stands at 0 and a checkered finish gate plus a checkered line stand at `length`. A progress strip along the top shows the 4 heads (via RR.Sprites.drawHead) from start to a finish flag, the player's head on a highlight pad, with heads in a tie nudged apart so all four stay visible. advanceTo glides all karts with easeInOutSine in 0.9-1.4 s (boost +15 % with soft speed lines), the camera trails the player by 140 ms then settles (so the player drifts a few px forward and back, never jerky), wheels toggle by speed up to 5.5 Hz, and a few small dust puffs fade behind moving karts. A second call snaps the running glide to its end first. The promise always resolves: on destroy, on a hidden tab, with reduced motion, or through a watchdog if frames never arrive. Idle drift runs at about 14.5 fps (timer plus rAF) only after setIdle(true) and stops when the tab is hidden; with idle off there is no rAF and no timer at all. Verified with scene-dev/test.cjs (ALL OK): API contract, fallbacks, junk arguments, plain-Node load, glide timing, boost, snap-on-second-call, destroy, hidden tab, reduced motion, idle cost, determinism, all 8 themes swept across the whole track with no render errors, off-screen karts not drawn, resize. Seams, road-vs-kart contrast and per-frame cost were also checked, and I looked at screenshots of every theme at the start, mid-race, near the finish and mid-glide.

## Limitations
- Only Chromium (Playwright) was tested. The code uses only basic Canvas 2D (fillRect, drawImage, getImageData is not used at runtime) and standard timers, so Safari and Firefox should be fine but are unverified.
- Karts at the same position are drawn diagonally fanned (4 px per lane) because of the oblique depth; two karts at nearly the same position can still overlap partly, and the back-lane heads are only partly visible behind the front kart. The strip nudges tied heads by up to about 9 px each (up to about 27 px for four at the start), so a head in a tie is not at its exact proportional x.
- Kart/road contrast is limited: Egel's orange body (L 63) against the road is only about 1.3-1.5:1 on most themes (1.2 on ice/hills). Karts stay readable because of the sprites' dark outlines, navy wheels (3.3-7.6:1) and hue differences, which I checked visually, but roads are mid-tone by design. Contrast numbers are in scene-dev/contrast.cjs.
- Landmarks are placed so everything is seen once during a 10-unit race (layer periods are about equal to the distance each layer scrolls). With a longer `length` than 10 the scenery repeats; with positions above about 11 the camera shows plain repeated scenery.
- Positions are clamped to 0..1000 and non-finite values count as 0. At most 4 racers are drawn (extras are ignored). setTrack does NOT reset positions and setRacers keeps the positions of ids it already knew, so the integrator should call place(...) after setTrack/setRacers at the start of a race. setRacers re-derives lanes: the player is always lane 0 and the others follow in the order of the lane numbers given.
- In reduced-motion mode advanceTo jumps instantly (the promise resolves at once, synchronously inside the call's microtask), so the UI should add its own short pause or fade if it wants one before showing the next sum.
- The wheel animation is distance/speed driven (2 sprite frames, at most 5.5 toggles per second), so it can look like slow-motion wheels at speed; a glide measured by the test can overshoot the nominal 1400 ms by one frame (about 1415-1430 ms).
- If the integrator scales the canvas by a non-integer factor, nearest-neighbour scaling gives pixels of uneven width; an integer multiple of 320 px wide (640, 960, 1280) is crispest.
- parts/scene.js is generated by `python3 scene-dev/build.py` from scene-dev/src/head.js, tail.js and src/themes/*.js; if someone edits parts/scene.js directly and reruns the build, the edits are lost. The preview page and tests load parts/scene.js.
- RR.Scene.onError (null by default) is a development hook that receives otherwise swallowed drawing errors and any unknown colour name; it is not part of the contract and the integrator can ignore it.

## Extra
LOGICAL SIZE: 320x144 (RR.Scene.SIZE = {width:320,height:144}; scene.width/height; create() sets canvas.width/height itself). Aspect ratio 20:9. No devicePixelRatio tricks, imageSmoothingEnabled=false, context made with {alpha:false}.

WORLD: RR.Scene.PX_PER_UNIT = 48 px per track unit (6.67 units visible across 320 px). Player reference point (front wheel) is at screen x=112, so the player kart centre is at x=104 (32.5 % of the width); kart centre = reference point - 8 px. Lane ground y (RR.Scene.LANE_Y) = [139,130,121,112] (lane 0 = player, front). Each lane further back is drawn 4 px more to the right (oblique depth); start/finish lines are slanted to match. Road is y 104-144, progress strip y 0-20, start line at world x=0, finish line at length*48 px. Positions above length (the RR.Race.rollout drive-on) work: karts continue past the line and the camera follows.

CAMERA: camera = player's position; during a glide it follows the player's own eased path delayed by 140 ms (so the player drifts a few px forward on screen, then settles back to x=104; max lag about 8 px), no extra settle tail: the glide ends exactly when the camera has arrived. Parallax layer factors are 0.04-0.06 (far), 0.18-0.22 (mid-far), 0.5-0.55 (mid), 1.0 (near/road); layer periods are chosen just above the distance each scrolls in one race (352-440, 576-608, 832 px) so every landmark is seen once and the tile seams never show.

TIMING: advanceTo total time = 900 ms for gaps up to 0.5 unit rising linearly to 1400 ms for 3+ units (camera settle included), x1.15 with {boost:true}. Rivals start/stop a hair apart per lane. Zero distance resolves at once without scheduling frames. Wheel frame toggles <= 5.5 Hz and only while a kart moves; dust puffs: 3-4 alive at a time, life 0.6 s; speed lines (boost) only behind the player, fading in and out with sin(pi*u).

IDLE: setIdle(true) -> setTimeout(66 ms) then one rAF per tick, about 14.5 fps measured; ambient time advances only then (and during glides) and drives cloud drift, windmill/turbine sails, gulls, kites, water sparkle, butterflies, ducks, skaters and steam. Idle off: measured 0 rAF and 0 timers. Hidden tab: loops cancelled, running glide jumps to its end and resolves; idle restarts on visibilitychange. Reduced motion (option, or scene.setReducedMotion(bool); if the option is omitted the scene reads prefers-reduced-motion and follows changes): advanceTo is instant, no wheel animation, no puffs/speed lines, no idle drift (setIdle has no effect).

EXTRA API (additive): scene.setReducedMotion(bool), scene.positions() (copy of the displayed positions), scene.length, scene.reducedMotion, RR.Scene.PX_PER_UNIT, RR.Scene.LANE_Y, RR.Scene.onError (dev hook). THEMES order: tulips, windmill, beach, forest, canal, harbour, ice, hills (same ids as RR.Tracks themes).

CSS THE INTEGRATOR SHOULD APPLY to the canvas: `canvas.scene { display:block; width:100%; height:auto; aspect-ratio:20/9; image-rendering:-moz-crisp-edges; image-rendering:crisp-edges; image-rendering:pixelated; }` inside a container whose width is preferably an integer multiple of 320 px (for example `width: min(100%, 960px)`; JS may floor the width to a multiple of 320 for perfectly even pixels). Give it role="img" and an aria-label in Dutch (the scene is decorative; the sums and positions must also be in the page text). The canvas should not be given a CSS background that shows through (it is opaque).

INTEGRATION CALL ORDER: scene = RR.Scene.create(canvas, {reducedMotion: matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)').matches}); scene.setTrack(track.theme, track.length); scene.setRacers(RR.Race.racers(state), playerHeroId); scene.place(RR.Race.positions(state)); after each right/wrong answer: await scene.advanceTo(RR.Race.positions(state), {boost: outcome === 'first'}); at the end drive on with scene.advanceTo(RR.Race.rollout(state)); on screen leave: scene.setIdle(false) / scene.destroy().
